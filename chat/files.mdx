## Attach a file and ask about it

<Steps>
  <Step title="Attach your file">
    Click **Add file**, the paperclip in the message box, and choose a file. Wait for the upload to finish.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Ask your question">
    Tell Scout what you want to understand. Name the sheet, page, or feature when you want it to focus on a particular part.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Send the message">
    Check that the attachment appears beside your draft, then send. If you attached several files, use their names to make your question clear.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Each file can be up to **50 MB**. To remove an attachment before sending, click its X in the attachment tray.

## Spreadsheets

**Formats:** Excel (`.xlsx`), CSV (`.csv`), and TSV (`.tsv`).

Ask Scout to explain how a workbook works: which cells supply a total, how sheets connect, and what assumptions are built into its formulas. For CSV and TSV files, ask about the columns and records you want to examine.

For example, to review a total in `Costing!F20`, ask the question below. Replace the sheet name and cell with the ones you want to examine in your workbook.

<Prompt description="Explain the total in cell F20 on the Costing sheet. Trace its formula to the input cells, including references to other sheets. Identify any inputs you could not read.">
  Explain the total in cell F20 on the Costing sheet. Trace its formula to the input cells, including references to other sheets. Identify any inputs you could not read.
</Prompt>

<Note>
  Scout reads up to the first **1,000 rows per Excel sheet** and the first **1,000 rows of a CSV or TSV**. Long content can be cut off earlier. Formulas are read as written, rather than recalculated.
</Note>

If the records you need are outside that coverage, attach a smaller file containing those records. For formula questions, keep the formulas and their referenced input cells together. Asking a narrower question does not expand the reading limit.

## PDFs

**Format:** `.pdf`.

Bring a report, specification, or drawing and ask about the parts that matter to your task. You can name a page, table, or section to focus the review.

<Prompt description="Summarize the key requirements in this document and point me to the relevant pages.">
  Summarize the key requirements in this document and point me to the relevant pages.
</Prompt>

## Images

**Formats:** `.png`, `.jpg`, `.jpeg`, `.gif`, and `.webp`.

Ask Scout about visible features, text, or annotations. For example: “Explain the annotations in this drawing and identify anything that is hard to read.”

## CAD models

**Formats:** STEP (`.step` and `.stp`).

Ask Scout to describe a part's geometry or focus on a particular feature. It uses the geometry and prepared views of the model to examine it.

<Prompt description="Describe the main geometric features of this part.">
  Describe the main geometric features of this part.
</Prompt>

## Keep working with a file

Continue in the same conversation when you want Scout to take a closer look. Refer to the filename, sheet, page, or feature in your follow-up. Scout can revisit the attachment with that focus; spreadsheet reading limits still apply.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I use the file in a new chat?">
    Attach it to the new chat. Files belong to the conversation where you uploaded them and do not automatically carry over.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="How do I use an updated version?">
    Attach the revised file and tell Scout which version to use.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can an attachment supply data to a dashboard?">
    Attachments are reference material for the conversation. They do not become connected database tables or inputs for dashboard refreshes.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

Click an uploaded filename on a sent message to download the original file.

For table downloads, see [Refresh or download a table](/chat/ask-questions/tables-and-charts#refresh-or-download-a-table). For upload errors, see [Troubleshooting](/help/troubleshooting#a-file-cannot-be-uploaded-or-fully-read).
